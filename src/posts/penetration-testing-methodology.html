My Penetration Testing Methodology

PENTESTING August 2026 Outline

A repeatable process, not a bag of tricks. This is the spine I run on every engagement — the write-up for each phase lands here as I fill it in.

Work in progress This page is the table of contents for now. Each section below is a placeholder I'll expand into method, tooling, and worked examples.

1 · Pre-Engagement

Scope, rules of engagement, authorization, exclusions, and what "success" means before a single packet goes out.

2 · Reconnaissance

Building the target picture, quietest sources first.

  • Passive — WHOIS, DNS, search-engine queries, web archive, social media, public code repositories, breach data.
  • Active — port scanning, service enumeration, banner grabbing, OS fingerprinting, network mapping, web spidering.

3 · Enumeration & Service Attacks

Turning open ports into a concrete attack surface, service by service: HTTP, FTP, SMB, SMTP, SSH.

4 · Vulnerability Assessment

CVE research, automated scanning versus manual validation, and prioritizing what's actually reachable and exploitable.

5 · Exploitation

Gaining the initial foothold — payload selection, getting a shell, and stabilizing it.

6 · Web Application Testing

Depth-first manual testing where scanners fall short.

  • Authentication & session management
  • Input validation — SQLi, XSS, SSTI, command injection, LFI
  • Access control — IDOR, forced browsing, privilege escalation
  • Business logic, API abuse, and file upload

7 · Post-Exploitation

Local enumeration, credential harvesting, and reaching the data that matters.

8 · Privilege Escalation

From foothold to full control on the host — Linux and Windows paths.

9 · Active Directory

The core of most internal engagements.

  • Enumeration — BloodHound, PowerView, LDAP
  • Kerberos attacks — Kerberoasting, AS-REP roasting
  • ACL & delegation abuse
  • Lateral movement — Pass-the-Hash, Pass-the-Ticket
  • Domain dominance — DCSync, Golden/Silver tickets

10 · Lateral Movement & Pivoting

Moving through the network — tunneling, pivoting, and reusing what's already been captured.

11 · Proof of Concept & Impact

Translating access into demonstrated business risk that engineers and executives can both act on.

12 · Reporting & Remediation

The deliverable — technical and executive, evidence-backed, mapped to OWASP / MITRE ATT&CK, prioritized by impact.

Appendix · Playbooks & References

Engagement-specific workflows underneath the process: external, internal, web-app, and cloud playbooks; a MITRE ATT&CK quick reference; and "things to keep in mind when you're stuck."
